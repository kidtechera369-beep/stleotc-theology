--- Slide 2 ---
The Orthodox Stance On Intercession
Our Belief:
The Ethiopian Orthodox Church believes in the intercession (prayer) of the Holy Virgin Mary, the Angels, and all the Saints.
The Common Objection:
"But doesn't the Bible say there is only 'one mediator'?"
1 Timothy 2:5: "For there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus."
1 John 2:1: "We have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous."
Our Answer:
Yes! And we 100% agree. The confusion comes from not understanding the
types
of mediation that exist .
--- Slide 3 ---
Two Types of "Advocacy"
Christ's Advocacy = PROPITIATORY
This is a big word that means
He paid the price for our sin.
His advocacy is based on His
sacrifice
and
His blood
.
He is the
only
one who can do this. He is our Redeemer.
The Saints' Intercession = SUPPLICATORY
This is a fancy word for
praying for someone.
Their intercession is
merely a prayer on our behalf
to Christ.
They are not redeeming us; they are
praying
for us.
--- Slide 4 ---
Christ: The Only Propitiatory Mediator
Scripture makes this clear. He is our Advocate
because
He is our propitiation (payment).
1 John 2:1-2:
"If any man sin, we have an advocate... Jesus Christ the righteous: And
He is the propitiation for our sins
..."
1 Timothy 2:5-6:
"...one mediator... Christ Jesus;
Who gave Himself a ransom for all
."
Key Lesson:
No saint, angel, or human can
pay
for your sins. Only Jesus Christ can. This is His unique, un-shareable role.
--- Slide 5 ---
God Loves and Asks For Intercessaction
The Old Testament is FILLED with examples of God requiring the intercession of His righteous ones as a condition for forgiveness.
This isn't to say God can't forgive directly. He does it to
honor His saints
and teach us about the power of prayer and unity.
Intercessacction is a particular example of how Orthodox christians derive their practices from the bible.
--- Slide 6 ---
Biblical Proof (Part 1): Abraham & Abimelech
God
Commands
Intercession
The Story (Genesis 20:1-7):
King Abimelech takes Sarah, not knowing she is Abraham's wife. God warns him in a dream.
God's Command:
"Now therefore restore the man his wife; for he is a prophet, and
he shall pray for thee, and thou shalt live
."
The Lesson:
God
Himself
made Abraham's prayer a condition for Abimelech's life.
--- Slide 7 ---
Biblical Proof (Part 2): Job & His Friends
God
Requires
Intercession
The Story (Job 42:7-8):
God is angry with Job's three friends for their foolish words.
God's Command:
"Therefore... go to my servant Job... and
my servant Job shall pray for you: for him will I accept
; lest I deal with you after your folly."
The Lesson:
God's forgiveness was tied directly to the intercession of His servant, Job.
--- Slide 8 ---
Biblical Proof (Part 3): Abraham & Sodom
Intercession "For the Sake of..."
The Story (Genesis 18:17-32):
God
tells Abraham
He is about to destroy Sodom, giving him the chance to intercede.
The Intercession:
Abraham bargains with God—"Wilt thou also destroy the righteous with the wicked?"
The Lesson:
God agrees: "If I find in Sodom fifty righteous... then
I will spare all the place for their sakes
." He would have saved the whole city "for the sake of" just ten righteous people.
--- Slide 9 ---
Biblical Proof (Part 4): Moses & Israel
Intercession Changes the Outcome
The Story (Exodus 32:7-14):
The people make the golden calf. God tells Moses He will destroy them.
The Intercession:
Moses pleads with God, "Turn from Thy fierce wrath... Remember Abraham, Isaac, and Israel, Thy servants..."
The Result:
"And
the Lord repented of the evil
which He thought to do unto His people." Moses' intercession saved them.
--- Slide 10 ---
What About Those Already in Heaven?
Does this power stop at death?
NO.
God continues to honor His saints long after they have departed.
Example: (1 Kings 11:12-13):
God is angry with Solomon. He says He will tear the kingdom away...
God's Promise:
"...notwithstanding in thy days I will not do it
for David thy father's sake
... I will give one tribe to thy son
for David my servant's sake
..."
King David was long departed, but God
still
showed mercy for his sake.
--- Slide 11 ---
A Question of Logic
Romans 15:30–32
— “I beseech you… that
ye strive together with me in your prayers to God for me
…”
If St. Paul, a living Saint, asked for the prayers of other living people
...
...and if we ask our friends and family (who are still striving and sinning) to pray for us…
Why would we
not
ask for the prayers of those who have
finished
their course, who are
perfected
in heaven, and who are
living in Paradise with Christ
?
Have they become
less
able to pray now that they are in God's presence? Or are they
more
able?
--- Slide 12 ---
Answering Common Questions (Part 1)
Question 1: "How can the saints in heaven even hear us?"
Answer:
Their knowledge is perfected. They know
more
in heaven, not less!
1 Corinthians 13:12:
"For now we see through a glass, darkly; but
then face to face: now I know in part; but then shall I know
even as also I am known."
They are not limited by a physical body.
Proof They Know What's Happening on Earth
Luke 15:10:
"Likewise, I say unto you, there is
joy in the presence of the angels of God over one sinner that repenteth
." (How can they rejoice if they don't know it happened?)
Revelation 5:8:
The 24 elders (saints) in heaven have "golden vials full of odours,
which are the prayers of the saints
." (How can they
offer
our prayers if they don't
receive
them?)
Luke 16:25-29:
In Paradise, Abraham knows all about the lives of both Lazarus and the rich man, and he even knows about "Moses and the prophets" who came hundreds of years after him.
--- Slide 13 ---
What About the Angels?
The Angels are also powerful intercessors.
Zechariah 1:12:
"Then the angel of the Lord... said:
O Lord of hosts, how long wilt Thou not have mercy on Jerusalem...
" (And God accepted his prayer!)
Hebrews 1:14:
Angels are called "ministering spirits, sent forth to minister for them who shall be heirs of salvation." They are actively working for us.
Genesis 48:16:
Jacob blesses Joseph's sons, saying, "
The Angel which redeemed me from all evil, bless the lads
."
--- Slide 14 ---
The Goal Behind
Intercession
The Goal is Theosis (Deification) -
Becoming
by grace
what God is
by nature
For us as Orthodox, the goal of salvation isn't just "getting into heaven" or "being forgiven." The goal is
Theosis
—to become "partakers of the divine nature" (2 Peter 1:4). God's plan is to make us
like Him
by grace.
How does intercession do this?
God is Love. He is a Trinity—a perfect communion of love between Father, Son, and Holy Spirit.
To become like God,
we
must become beings of perfect love.
Sin is selfishness and isolation. Love is the opposite: it is relational and self-giving.
When God invites us to pray for each other, He is
training us in love
. He is calling us out of our selfish isolation to participate in the care of others. When we intercede, we are acting like God, who cares for all.
--- Slide 15 ---
Summary: Why We Ask for Intercession
1. We do NOT "pray to" them.
We
ask for their prayers
(their intercession), just as we ask our friends on earth.
2. To Honor Them.
God Himself honors them by accepting their prayers. "If any man serve me, him will my Father honour." (John 12:26)
3. Because They Are ALIVE.
We believe in the "life of the world to come."
4. For Humility.
We show humility by admitting we need the prayers of those who are holier than us.
5. For Unity.
We believe in
One Church
—the Body of Christ. This body is not broken by death. We are all connected. They pray for us, and we honor them. It is the perfect communion of saints.
Lastly, Because of Theosis.
