What Does “Hypostasis” Mean?
Hypostasis is a Greek theological term.
In Orthodox teaching, it refers to a real personal distinction within the One God.
It can be understood as a distinct personal reality or personal property within the one divine nature.
The Three Hypostases are not separate gods; they are the eternal personal realities of the One God.
The Father • The Son • The Holy Spirit
Scripture references: OSB/NKJV wording where quoted • Source: Week 19 lesson article, “Trinitarianism and Monotheism.”

The Three Hypostases
The Father
The origin, source, and fountainhead.
The Son
The Word, the divine intellect, begotten of the Father.
The Holy Spirit
The giver of life, proceeding from the Father, the Spirit of life.
These names are not human inventions. Christ Himself gives them in Matthew 28:19.

--- Slide 10 ---
No Separation in the Trinity
The Father, Son, and Holy Spirit are distinct, but never separated.
The Son is in the Father. The Father is in the Son. The Holy Spirit is with the Father and the Son.
The Three are always united in essence, will, power, and divine action.
“I am in the Father, and the Father in Me.”
John 14:10
Scripture references: OSB/NKJV wording where quoted • Source: Week 19 lesson article, “Trinitarianism and Monotheism.”
--- Slide 11 ---
No One Comes Before the Other in Time
The Father never existed without the Son or the Holy Spirit.
There was never a time when the Father was without His Word or without His Spirit.
There was never a time when the Son or the Holy Spirit did not exist.
Like a candle: flame and light come together; neither precedes the other.
Likewise, the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit are eternal together.

Common Misunderstandings to Avoid
False: Christians believe in three gods.
Truth: We believe in One God.
False: The Three are just three names for one Person.
Truth: They are three real Hypostases.
False: The Son and Holy Spirit are created beings.
Truth: They are eternal and divine.
False: The Trinity can be fully explained by human logic.
Truth: The Trinity is a divine mystery revealed by God.

The Foundation — The Nicene Creed
The Nicene Creed:
“We believe in one God, God the Father Almighty, Maker of heaven and earth, and of all things visible and invisible.
And in one Lord Jesus Christ, the only-begotten Son of God, begotten of the Father before all worlds; Light of Light, very God of very God, begotten, not made, being of one essence with the Father; by whom all things were made;
Who for us men and for our salvation came down from heaven, and was incarnate of the Holy Spirit and of the Virgin Mary, and became man;
And was crucified for us under Pontius Pilate, and suffered and was buried;
And the third day He rose again, according to the Scriptures;
And ascended into heaven, and sits at the right hand of the Father;
And He shall come again with glory to judge the living and the dead; whose kingdom shall have no end.
And we believe in the Holy Spirit, the Lord, the Giver of Life, who proceeds from the Father; who with the Father and the Son together is worshipped and glorified; who spoke by the prophets.
And in one holy catholic and apostolic Church.
We acknowledge one baptism for the remission of sins.
We look for the resurrection of the dead, and the life of the world to come. Amen.”
The Creed was established during two major Church councils:
First Council of Nicaea
First Council of Constantinople
The purpose was to:
Defend the truth about Christ
Reject false teachings (like denying Christ’s divinity)
Unite the Church in one clear belief
The Nicene Creed is the official statement
of faith of the Church.
It clearly defines what we believe about God, especially about: The Trinity.
(Excerpt from the Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed)

Practical Application
Because God is Trinity:
We worship one God with reverence:
Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, one in essence and glory.
We pray in a Trinitarian way:
to the Father, through the Son, in the Holy Spirit.
We live in communion:
because God Himself is eternal love and communion.
We guard the true faith:
rejecting confusion, false teaching, and the idea of “three gods.”
We confess with confidence:
the faith revealed by Christ, taught by the apostles, and preserved by the Church.
We seek holiness:
allowing the Holy Spirit to sanctify us and draw us into the life of God.
