Context And Background
Author:
St. Paul the Apostle
Contex
t

Written c. 60–62 AD during his first imprisonment in Rome; one of the "Prison Epistles."
Audience:
The local Church in Ephesus and an

To reveal the Church not merely as a group, but as the

To instruct the baptized in the

The Ascetic Struggle:
To equip the faithful for
Spiritual Warfare

--- Slide 4 ---
The Church as Body and Bride
Paul uses "Theophanic" images to show that salvation is a communal reality, not a private one.
The Body and the Head
(
Eph 1:22–23
)
Theological Point:
The Church is the "Fullness" of Christ. As a body cannot live without a head, and a head is not complete without its members, Christ chooses to be "completed" by us.
The Bride and the Bridegroom
(
Eph 5:23–32
)
Theological Point:
This is the "Great Mystery." It reveals that the goal of human life is a
Sacramental Union
. Just as a husband and wife become "one flesh," the Church becomes "one spirit" with Christ.
Bible verses (KJV):
Ephesians 1:22–23
“And hath put all things under his feet, and gave him to be the head over all things to the church,
Which is his body, the
fullness
of him that filleth all in all.”
Ephesians 5:23
“For the husband is the head of the wife, even as Christ is the head of the church: and he is the saviour of the body.”
The Definition
A
theophany
is a "God-showing." When we say an image is "theophanic," we mean it isn't just a metaphor or a pretty picture; it is a description of how God’s
Uncreated Light
and presence break into our world.
--- Slide 5 ---
The Body and the Head: Organic Unity
While we often think of "the Body" as a metaphor for teamwork, the biblical context is more
organic
and
vital
.
Life-Source:
A body without a head is a corpse. By calling Christ the "Head," Paul is saying that the Church’s very life, direction, and "nervous system" come from Him.
Interdependence:
In a body, a toe cannot claim to be independent of the lungs. This shatters the idea of "private" Christianity. If one part of the body suffers, the whole system feels the stress.
The "Fullness":
Ephesians 1:23 calls the Church "the fullness of him who fills all in all." This suggests that Christ chooses to manifest His presence on Earth
through
this collective group, rather than appearing as a solo actor.
--- Slide 6 ---
The Bride and the Bridegroom: Covenantal Intimacy
If the "Body" emphasizes
unity and function
, the "Bride" emphasizes
love and identity
.
Sacrificial Devotion:
Paul points out that Christ "gave himself up" for the Bride (Eph 5:25). This moves salvation away from a legal transaction and into the realm of a marriage covenant.
The Process of Sanctification:
The text describes Christ "washing" the Bride with the word to present her "without stain or wrinkle." This shows that the Church’s holiness isn't a DIY project; it is the result of Christ’s ongoing care for His people as a whole.
The "Great Mystery":
Paul explicitly says that human marriage is a "signpost" pointing toward this deeper reality. The ultimate goal of history is the union of the Creator with His creation.
In the
King James Version (KJV)
, Ephesians 5:25 reads:
"Husbands, love your wives, even as Christ also loved the church, and gave himself for it;"

--- Slide 9 ---
The Structure Of Ephesians
Ch. 1–3:
Identity (What God has done in Christ)
Ch. 4–5:
Life (How the Church must live)
Ch. 6:
Spiritual
Warfare (How believers stand and endure)
--- Slide 10 ---
1. Chapters 1–3: The Divine Identity (The "Indicative")
Before St. Paul gives a single command, he spends three chapters explaining what God has already finished.
The Theology:
You are adopted, redeemed, and sealed.
The Shift:
We move from being "dead in trespasses" to being seated in "heavenly places."
The Goal:
To understand that the Church is not a human invention, but a divine mystery revealed.
--- Slide 11 ---
Chapters 4–5: The Christian Life (The "Imperative")
Key Verse: Ephesians 4:1
—
"I therefore... beseech you that ye walk worthy of the vocation wherewith ye are called."
The Transition:
The word "therefore" is the hinge. Because of who you
are
(Ch. 1–3), this is how you must
live
(Ch. 4–5).
The Practice:
This isn't about "getting" saved; it's about "walking" out the salvation you already have. It covers unity, purity, and the "Body and Bride" relationships in the home and church.
--- Slide 12 ---
Chapter 6: The Spiritual Warfare (The "Endurance")
Key Verse: Ephesians 6:10–11
—
"Finally, my brethren, be strong in the Lord... Put on the whole armour of God."
The Reality:
A "worthy walk" inevitably triggers opposition.
The Strategy:
Notice that the armor is mostly defensive. The goal isn't to "win" a new victory (Christ already did that in Ch. 1), but to
stand
and hold the ground Christ has already won.
