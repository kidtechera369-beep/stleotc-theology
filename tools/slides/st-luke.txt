Who Was Saint Luke?
Not One of the Twelve Apostles:
Luke was not among the original twelve apostles of Jesus. Instead, he is believed to have been a Gentile convert.
A Physician:
Colossians 4:14 refers to him as “the beloved physician” (ὁ ἰατρὸς ὁ ἀγαπητὸς).
Companion of Saint Paul:
He traveled with Saint Paul on his missionary journeys and was one of Paul's most trusted followers. In
2 Timothy 4:11
, Paul states,
“Only Luke is with me.”
Highly Educated:
His writings in Greek are among the most refined in the New Testament, suggesting he was well-educated.

S
aint Luke’s Gospel and Its Unique Characteristics
The
Gospel of Luke
is the third book in the New Testament and has
distinct themes:
Saint Luke provided the world with a well-researched and orderly account of Jesus Christ’s ancestry, birth, and earthly life, followed by a structured narrative of His ministry, highlighting key teachings and events.
Focus on Christ’s Compassion:
Luke’s Gospel emphasizes Jesus’ love for sinners, the poor, women, and Gentiles.
A Universal Gospel:
Unlike Matthew, which was written for a Jewish audience, Luke’s Gospel presents Jesus as the Savior of
all
people, both Jews and Gentiles.
Emphasis on Prayer and the Holy Spirit:
Luke frequently mentions Jesus praying and emphasizes the work of the Holy Spirit.

, also written by Saint Luke, is a continuation of his Gospel. It details:

St. Luke

Great Commission

The Martyrdom of St. Luke
After the martyrdom of St. Peter and St. Paul, St. Luke continued preaching in Rome, leading many to Christ.
Idol worshippers and non-believing Jews reported him to Emperor Nero, leading to his imprisonment.
Before his capture, he entrusted his writings to a fisherman, saying they would lead him to God’s way.
Nero ordered Luke’s hand severed, but he miraculously reattached it and then separated it again, leading 276 people, including the Emperor’s cabinet head and wife, to believe in Christ.
Enraged, Nero ordered St. Luke and the new converts to be beheaded, granting him the crown of martyrdom on 22nd Tahisas (December 31).
His body was thrown into the sea in a sack but was later found and buried with great honor on an island by a believer.
