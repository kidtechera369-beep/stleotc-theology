--- Slide 7 ---
The Cost of Our Forgiveness
This was not a simple act. The cost of our sin was paid by Christ in every conceivable way—spiritually, psychologically, and physically—to
redeem humanity
.
1. The Spiritual Agony (Gethsemane)
The suffering began before the physical torture. Christ took upon Himself the full weight, horror, and filth of every sin committed.
Anguish:
The anguish was so profound that His body reacted with
Hematidrosis
—sweating "great drops of blood." - 2 Corinthians 5:21
2. The Physical Torture (The Praetorium)
The goal of the Roman scourging was to bring the victim to the point of death.
The Scourging:
He was beaten with the Roman
flagrum
(a whip with bone and metal).
Beatings and Slaps:
He endured countless slaps and beatings from the temple guards and Roman soldiers, often focused on His face and head.
The Public Humiliation (Golgotha)
The Crowning:
A thick "crown" of sharp thorns was pressed into His scalp, mocking His Kingship and inflicting continuous pain.
Humiliation:
He was stripped naked, mocked, and forced to carry the cross. Crucifixion was the ultimate death of shame, reserved for traitors and the worst criminals.
The Death:
He was pierced with nails, suffering hours of agonizing asphyxiation, having to push up on the nails just to breathe.
Verse:
"But He was wounded for our transgressions, He was bruised for our iniquities; the chastisement for our peace was upon Him, and by His stripes we are healed."
-
Isaiah 53:5

Too Much To Ask?
God asks us to love Him and love others. This dual love is the central requirement of the Christian life.
Question for Reflection:
Having seen the depths of His love, is love
too much to ask
?
Verse:
"Jesus said to him, ‘You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, and with all your soul, and with all your mind.’ This is the first and great commandment. And the second is like it: ‘You shall love your neighbor as yourself.’" -
Matthew 22:37-39
Our "suffering" is the daily, necessary process of
dying to self
—crucifying the flesh and resisting the desires that keep us separated from God.
Question for Reflection:
Can the suffering of trying to abstain from sin be equated to His suffering?
Denying our pride, our anger, and our worldly desires.
Fasting, Prayer, Early Liturgy
This struggle is our
necessary
participation
in the
principle
of His great love. It is the cost of becoming God-like (Theosis).
Verse:
"I have been crucified with Christ; it is no longer I who live, but Christ lives in me..."-
Galatians 2:20
