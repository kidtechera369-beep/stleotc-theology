The Foundation — The Nicene Creed
The Nicene Creed:
“We believe in one God, God the Father Almighty, Maker of heaven and earth, and of all things visible and invisible.
And in one Lord Jesus Christ, the only-begotten Son of God, begotten of the Father before all worlds; Light of Light, very God of very God, begotten, not made, being of one essence with the Father; by whom all things were made;
Who for us men and for our salvation came down from heaven, and was incarnate of the Holy Spirit and of the Virgin Mary, and became man;
And was crucified for us under Pontius Pilate, and suffered and was buried;
And the third day He rose again, according to the Scriptures;
And ascended into heaven, and sits at the right hand of the Father;
And He shall come again with glory to judge the living and the dead; whose kingdom shall have no end.
And we believe in the Holy Spirit, the Lord, the Giver of Life, who proceeds from the Father; who with the Father and the Son together is worshipped and glorified; who spoke by the prophets.
And in one holy catholic and apostolic Church.
We acknowledge one baptism for the remission of sins.
We look for the resurrection of the dead, and the life of the world to come. Amen.”
The Creed was established during two major Church councils:
First Council of Nicaea
First Council of Constantinople
The purpose was to:
Defend the truth about Christ
Reject false teachings (like denying Christ’s divinity)
Unite the Church in one clear belief
The Nicene Creed is the official statement
of faith of the Church.
It clearly defines what we believe about God, especially about: The Trinity.
(Excerpt from the Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed)
--- Slide 3 ---
One Divine Nature — The
Essence
In theology, "Nature" or "Essence" refers to the shared substance of God. It is what makes God, God.
Indivisibility:
There is only
one
Divine Nature. It cannot be divided or shared like a physical object.
Shared Attributes:
Everything that belongs to the Godhead is possessed fully by all three Persons:
Eternity:
All three are without beginning or end.
Omnipotence:
All three are Almighty; they possess all power.
Omniscience:
All three know all things (past, present, and future).
Holiness:
All three are the source of all holiness.
One Will & One Action:
They act as one God with one divine will, without confusion of the Persons
--- Slide 4 ---
Three Distinct Persons — The
Person
While the Nature is One, the Persons are Three. We distinguish them only by their eternal relationships.
The Father (The Source):
He is the "Source(Cause)" He is
Unbegotten
, meaning He has no cause and comes from no one.
The Son (The Word):
He is eternally
Begotten
of the Father. He is the Word and Wisdom of God.
The Holy Spirit (The Life-Giver):
He eternally
Proceeds
from the Father. He is the Breath of God.
Equality:
One Person is not "more God" than the others. They are co-equal in majesty, glory, and worship.
The Father is the
origin within the Trinity
The Son comes from the Father by
begetting
The Holy Spirit comes from the Father by
procession
This is called the
“Monarchy of the Father”
in Orthodox theology

Deep Dive —
Terms
Unbegotten (The Father):
This means "Without Cause." The Father is the only Person who does not come from anyone else. He is the "Source" (
Arche
) of the Godhead.
Begotten (The Son):
This describes an
Eternal Birth
.
Not a Creation:
A child is "begotten" (same nature as the parent), but a house is "made" (different nature).
Outside of Time:
Just as a thought is born in the mind the moment the mind exists, the Son is born of the Father eternally.
Proceeds (The Spirit):
This describes a
Spiritual Flow
.
The Breath:
Just as breath "proceeds" from a person's lungs, the Spirit proceeds from the Father.
The Only Source:
We believe the Spirit proceeds
only
from the Father. To say He proceeds from the Son (the
Filioque
heresy) would suggest there are two sources in the Trinity, which we reject.
Co-Existent (Together-Eternal):
None of the Persons existed before the others. The Father was never without His Word (Son) or His Breath (Spirit).
These are eternal relationships, not events in time
Understanding these technical terms helps us avoid heresy.

Deep Dive — Begotten, Proceeds, Co-Existent
1. Unbegotten: The "Source" (The Father)
Think of a
Mountain Spring.
The water is just
there
. It didn't flow from another pipe or another mountain. It is the
Source.
In plain English:
"Unbegotten" simply means the Father is the
Start.
He doesn't have a "source" because He
is
the source. He is the Mind that thinks the Thought.
2. Begotten: The "Birth of the Word" (The Son)
Think of a
Thought in your mind.
The moment you have a thought, it is "born" in your head. It didn't take time to grow; it just appeared because you are a thinking being.
In plain English:
"Begotten" means the Son comes from the Father like a
Word
comes from a
Thought.
Why does this matter?
Because a word has the same "stuff" as the thought. If you think about an apple, your word is "apple." They are the same nature. The Son is the "Word" of God, meaning He is exactly like the Father.
3. Proceeding: The "Flow of Life" (The Holy Spirit)
Think of your
Breath.
When you speak a word, your breath carries that word out into the room. You don't "give birth" to your breath like a baby; it
flows out
of you.
In plain English:
"Proceeding" means the Spirit flows from the Father like
Breath
or
Energy.
*
Why the difference?
We use a different word ("Proceeding") because the Spirit relates to the Father differently than the Son does. If the Son is the
Word
God speaks, the Spirit is the
Life/Breath
that makes the Word alive.
--- Slide 8 ---
Deep Dive —
Unbegotten: The “Source: (The Father)
Unbegotten
means the Father does not come from anyone else.
He is not born from another.
He is not made by another.
He is not caused by another.
He is not created by another.
In
plain English:
the Father has no source because He is the Source.
This does
not
mean the Father existed before the Son or before the Holy Spirit. God is not trapped inside time like we are.
The Father, Son, and Holy Spirit are all eternal. There was never a time when the Father was without His Son or without His Spirit.
The Father is called
Unbegotten
because He is the eternal Source within the Holy Trinity.
So when we say the Father is “the Source,” we are not saying He is greater in nature than the Son or the Holy Spirit. We are saying this is what makes Him personally the Father.
The Father is fully God.
The Son is fully God.
The Holy Spirit is fully God.
The difference is not in their divinity. The difference is in their personal relationship to one another.

“

When you speak a word, that word comes from inside you. It is not something outside of you that you built with your hands. It comes from your mind and expresses what is already in you.
So when we call Jesus the Word of God, we mean the Son perfectly reveals who the Father is. He is not a separate kind of being. He is not less than the Father. NATURE:
A Human begets a Human. Therefore, God the Father begets God the Son. They are made of the same Divine Nature.
It is NOT "Making":
God

It is ETERNAL:
Think of Light coming from a Flame. The light is "born" from the flame, but they both start at the exact same time. There was never a Flame without its Light.

The Son is the Father’s perfect "Self-Expression." Everything the Father is, the Son is—except for the fact that He is the Son and not the Father.
To

To
BEGET

is to produce something that is the
exact same

--- Slide 10 ---
Deep Dive —
Proceeding The Holy Spirit
Proceeding
describes the Holy Spirit’s eternal relationship to the Father.
The Holy Spirit is not created.
He is not made.
He is not begotten like the Son.
He eternally proceeds from the Father.
In plain English:
“Proceeding” means the Spirit flows from the Father like Breath or Energy.
Why the difference?
We use a different word (“Proceeding”) because the Spirit relates to the Father differently than the Son does. If the Son is the Word God speaks, the Spirit is the Life/Breath that makes the Word alive.
This does
not
mean the Holy Spirit is just air, energy, or a force.
He is a
divine Person
, fully God, eternal, and one in essence with the Father and the Son.
The Son is
begotten
from the Father. The Holy Spirit
proceeds
from the Father.
These are not two ways of being created. They are two eternal relationships inside the Holy Trinity.
Bottom Line
The Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father, gives life, and is worshiped together with the Father and the Son as one God.
--- Slide 11 ---
One Essence, Three Eternal Relationships
The Father alone is unbegotten.
The Son is eternally begotten of the Father.
The Holy Spirit eternally proceeds from the Father.
These three terms identify the eternal personal distinctions in the Trinity:
Unbegotten
distinguishes the Father
Begotten
distinguishes the Son
Proceeds
distinguishes the Holy Spirit
The Son and Spirit are both fully God and both from the Father, but they are not the same Person because their origin from the Father is not identical:
the Son is from the Father by begetting
the Spirit is from the Father by procession
The Church confesses this distinction, but does not reduce it to biology, mechanics, or material imagery.

The Two Hands of God — Creation
In the teaching of the Church Fathers, especially St. Irenaeus, the Son and the Holy Spirit are called the “Two Hands” of the Father. This teaches that the one God creates and acts through His Word and His Spirit.
The Word:
“By the Word of the Lord the heavens were made…” (Psalm 33:6)
The Breath:
“…and all the host of them by the Breath of His mouth.” (Psalm 33:6)
The Formation of Man:
“Then the Lord God formed man of the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living being.” (Genesis 2:7)
“In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.” “All things were made through Him, and without Him nothing was made that was made.” (John 1:1, 3)
The Action:
The Father creates through His Word (the Son) and gives life through His Breath (the Holy Spirit).
Important
:
This image helps us understand God’s work, but it does not fully explain the mystery of the Trinity.

The New Creation:

The Incarnation:

The

The Point:
There is no separation. In every act of God—from creating the stars to the birth of Christ—all three Persons are present and active as One.

The Analogy of Fire
The Church Fathers use this to help us visualize the Trinity without dividing the Godhead.
The Flame (The Father):
The flame is the source. It represents the Father as the "Root" or "Cause."
The Light (The Son):
As soon as the candle is lit, light is born (
begotten
) from the flame. You cannot have the flame without the light.
The Heat (The Holy Spirit):
The heat comes forth (
proceeds
) from the flame. It is distinct from the light, yet inseparable from the flame.
The Lesson:
Unity:
They are one single fire.
Simultaneity:
The light and heat do not wait to appear; they exist as soon as the flame exists (
Co-eternity
).
This is only an analogy — God is beyond all comparison

--- Slide 17 ---
Conclusion — The Tewahedo Mystery
Unified without Confusion:
The Father is not the Son, and the Son is not the Spirit. We do not "mix" the Persons (rejecting Modalism).
Distinguished without Separation:
They are never "apart." Where one is, the others are also (rejecting Tritheism).
Our Worship:
We do not master this mystery with logic; we enter into it through prayer and the Sacraments.
Doxology:
"Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever and unto the ages of ages. Amen."
