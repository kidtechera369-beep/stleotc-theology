--- Slide 2 ---
Context and Background
Author:
St. Paul the Apostle
Audience:
the church in Colossae
Purpose:
To proclaim Christ’s supremacy and sufficiency, warn against deception and man-made religion, and call the Church to live a transformed, Christ-centered life rooted in Him.
Colossians 1:1–2 (NKJV)
“Paul, an apostle of Jesus Christ by the will of God, and Timothy our brother, To the saints and faithful brethren in Christ who are in Colossae: Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.”
--- Slide 3 ---
The Big Message of Colossians
The Big Message of Colossians
1) Supremacy:
Christ is Creator/Sustainer, Head of the Church, fullness of God (Col 1:16–18; 2:9)
2) Reconciliation:
Christ made peace through His blood and restored us to God (Col 1:20–22)
3) Stay Rooted:
Don’t be deceived; you are complete in Him (Col 2:8; 2:10)
4) New Life:
Set your mind above; put off sin; put on Christ; live prayerfully with grace-filled speech (Col 3:1–2, 5, 12–14; 4:2–6)
Core takeaway:
Colossians proclaims Christ as supreme and sufficient—so we stay rooted in Him and live a transformed life.
--- Slide 4 ---
Staying Rooted In Christ
Why Paul says this (what was happening)
Some people were pushing ideas that sounded “deep” and “spiritual” but subtly changed the center:
Christ + extra rules
(man-made religion)
Christ + mystical experiences
(spiritual pride)
Christ + philosophies/traditions
(human wisdom)
Christ reduced
(as if He’s not fully God / not enough)
Core takeaway:
If Christ is reduced, the entire faith collapses into confusion.
--- Slide 5 ---
Christ + extra rules (man-made religion)
What it looks like:
Someone treats fasting as the whole faith:
“If you didn’t fast perfectly, you’re basically not Christian,” while they ignore humility, mercy, confession, and love.
Making personal opinions into law:
“If you don’t do
exactly
my rule (my prayer count, my strictness, my schedule), you’re wrong,” even when the Church gives guidance through a priest/spiritual father.
Weaponizing canons instead of using them as medicine:
quoting rules only to condemn, rather than to heal and restore.
Turning external correctness into righteousness:
“I wear this, I don’t go there, I don’t listen to that,” but there’s no repentance, no forgiveness, no purity of heart.
The Colossians correction:
Rules without Christ-centered repentance create pride and harsh judgment.
--- Slide 6 ---
Christ + mystical experiences (spiritual pride)
What it looks like:
Chasing “feelings” or “spiritual highs” in worship:
“If I didn’t feel chills/tears today, God wasn’t present.”
Bragging about spiritual experiences:
dreams, visions, “God told me…” used to impress people or claim authority.
Seeking “secret knowledge” or dramatic signs:
constantly looking for miracles, prophecies, or “deep revelations,” while neglecting basic obedience, prayer, and humility.
Thinking spiritual maturity = exotic experiences:
instead of
repentance, patience, forgiveness, and purity
.
Orthodox caution (very traditional):
The Fathers constantly warn that chasing experiences can lead to
prelest
(spiritual delusion). True spirituality is usually quiet, humble, and obedient.
The Colossians correction:
Christ is the treasure, not the experience. Holiness is proved by humility, not stories.
--- Slide 7 ---
Christ + philosophies/traditions (human wisdom)
This one needs precision because Orthodoxy loves
Holy Tradition
—but the danger is
human tradition
or outside philosophies becoming the real authority.
What it looks like:
Treating ethnic/cultural tradition as equal to the Gospel:
“We do it because our people do it,” even if it contradicts humility and love.
Replacing Christ with “identity Orthodoxy”:
being Orthodox as a cultural badge (“we are the true ones”) rather than a life of repentance and communion with Christ.
Importing non-Christian spiritual frameworks as the engine:
“Orthodoxy + manifestation, astrology, crystals, energy rituals, New Age stuff,” mixing systems that shift trust away from Christ.
Over-intellectualizing faith:
using philosophy to win arguments and flex knowledge, while neglecting prayer and obedience.
The Colossians correction:
Human wisdom becomes dangerous when it starts
ruling
the heart and displacing Christ.
--- Slide 8 ---
Christ reduced (as if He’s not fully God / not enough)
What it looks like:
Treating Jesus like just a moral teacher:
“He’s a great example,” but not the living Lord, Savior, and God.
Treating the sacraments as symbolic only:
reducing the Church to ideas instead of the real life of Christ given to us.
Approaching saints/icons like “alternatives” to Christ instead of witnesses to Him:
asking for help from saints but not repenting, not praying, not seeking Christ.
(Orthodox view: saints intercede
because of Christ
, never as replacements for Him.)
Living like Christ isn’t enough to heal sin:
“Christ forgives me, but I still need my vice to cope,” so the heart leans on lust, alcohol, validation, money, etc. as the real savior.
The Colossians correction (simple):
If Christ is truly the fullness of God, then you don’t need another savior.
--- Slide 9 ---
Paul’s Prayer for Real Growth
Paul prays for wisdom, understanding, and strength.
True growth is endurance, fruit, and a life that pleases God.
Spiritual maturity looks like patience and steady obedience.
Colossians 1:9–10 (NKJV)
“For this reason we also, since the day we heard it, do not cease to pray for you, and to ask that you may be filled with the knowledge of His will in all wisdom and spiritual understanding; that you may walk worthy of the Lord, fully pleasing Him, being fruitful in every good work and increasing in the knowledge of God.”
Core takeaway:
Real growth is a life that pleases God—steady, fruitful, enduring.
--- Slide 10 ---
Christ Reveals the Invisible God
Christ is the perfect revelation of God.
He is not a created being.
If you want to know God, look at Christ
.
Colossians 1:15 (NKJV)
“He is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn over all creation.”
Core takeaway:
To know God truly, look at Christ.
--- Slide 11 ---
Christ Created All Things
Christ is Creator, not created.
Everything exists through Him and for Him.
If all things are for Him, your life is for Him too.
Colossians 1:16 (NKJV)
“For by Him all things were created that are in heaven and that are on earth, visible and invisible, whether thrones or dominions or principalities or powers. All things were created through Him and for Him.”
Core takeaway:
All creation is through Him and for Him—your life included.
--- Slide 12 ---
Christ Holds Everything Together
Christ is not only Creator, but Sustainer.
Without Christ, everything collapses.
If He holds creation together, He can hold you together.
Colossians 1:17 (NKJV)
“And He is before all things, and in Him all things consist.”
Core takeaway:
If He holds all things together, He can hold you together.
--- Slide 13 ---
Christ Reconciles Us by His Blood
Salvation is not self-improvement
alone
.
We were enemies, now reconciled.
Christ makes peace through the cross.
Colossians 1:20 (NKJV)
“And by Him to reconcile all things to Himself, by Him, whether things on earth or things in heaven, having made peace through the blood of His cross.”
Core takeaway:
Peace with God comes through the cross—Christ made peace by His blood.
--- Slide 14 ---
Beware of Teachings That Capture You
False teaching can sound wise and spiritual.
But it pulls you away from Christ’s sufficiency.
Not everything that sounds spiritual is from God.
Colossians 2:8 (NKJV)
“Beware lest anyone cheat you through philosophy and empty deceit, according to the tradition of men, according to the basic principles of the world, and not according to Christ.”
Core takeaway:
Not everything that sounds spiritual is from God—measure it by Christ.
--- Slide 15 ---
You Are Complete in Christ
You don’t need “Christ + something else.”
Christ is enough.
The enemy wants you to feel incomplete without validation.
Colossians 2:10 (NKJV)
“And you are complete in Him, who is the head of all principality and power.”
Core takeaway:
You are complete in Christ—stop living like you’re missing something.
--- Slide 16 ---
Christ Is the Substance
Do not let people replace Christ with performance.
Shadows point to Christ — He is the reality.
Keep Christ at the center of worship and life.
Colossians 2:16–17 (NKJV)
“So let no one judge you in food or in drink, or regarding a festival or a new moon or sabbaths, which are a shadow of things to come, but the substance is of Christ.”
Core takeaway:
Don’t trade Christ for external performance—He is the substance.
--- Slide 17 ---
Set Your Mind Above
A new life needs a new focus.
Where the mind goes, the life follows.
Holiness begins with what you seek.
Colossians 3:1–2 (NKJV)
“If then you were raised with Christ, seek those things which are above, where Christ is, sitting at the right hand of God. Set your mind on things above, not on things on the earth.”
Core takeaway:
Holiness starts with what you seek and set your mind on.
--- Slide 18 ---
Put to Death the Old Life
Paul is direct: put sin to death.
Sin is not a pet; it’s a disease.
Grace gives power to kill sin.
Colossians 3:5 (NKJV)
“Therefore put to death your members which are on the earth: fornication, uncleanness, passion, evil desire, and covetousness, which is idolatry.”
Core takeaway:
Grace empowers you to kill sin, not manage it.
--- Slide 19 ---
Put Off /
Put On
Put
off
anger, wrath, malice, and lying.
Put on mercy, kindness, humility, patience.
Holiness is not only what you stop — it’s what you become.
Colossians 3:12 (NKJV)
“Therefore, as the elect of God, holy and beloved, put on tender mercies, kindness, humility, meekness, longsuffering.”
Core takeaway:
Holiness is not only what you stop—it’s what you become.
--- Slide 20 ---
Love and Peace Hold the Church Together
Love is the bond of perfection.
Peace must rule the heart.
Unity is spiritual discipline.
Colossians 3:14–15 (NKJV)
“But above all these things put on love, which is the bond of perfection. And let the peace of God rule in your hearts, to which also you were called in one body; and be thankful.”
Core takeaway:
Love and peace are the marks of maturity in one Body.
Love is the “tie” that holds every Christian virtue together and brings them to maturity.
Paul just listed virtues like mercy, kindness, humility, meekness, patience, forgiveness (Col 3:12–13). Those virtues can exist in a small, incomplete way—
but love binds them into one unified, mature life.
“Bond” = what holds it together

--- Slide 22 ---
The Word and Worship in Daily Life
Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly.
Teach, exhort, and worship with wisdom.
A strong Christian is full of Scripture and praise.
Colossians 3:16 (NKJV)
“Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly in all wisdom, teaching and admonishing one another in psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, singing with grace in your hearts to the Lord.”
Core takeaway:
A strong Christian is full of Scripture and worship.
--- Slide 23 ---
Whatever You Do — Do It in Jesus’ Name
Faith is not only Sunday.
Your work, speech, and actions matter.
If Christ is Lord, He is Lord of your Monday too.
Colossians 3:17 (NKJV)
“And whatever you do in word or deed, do all in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks to God the Father through Him.”
Core takeaway:
If Christ is Lord, He is Lord of your Monday—every word and deed.
--- Slide 24 ---
Prayer and Watchfulness
Prayer must be consistent and awake.
Watchfulness is spiritual seriousness.
Prayer opens doors for the Gospel.
Colossians 4:2–3 (NKJV)
“Continue earnestly in prayer, being vigilant in it with thanksgiving; meanwhile praying also for us, that God would open to us a door for the word, to speak the mystery of Christ, for which I am also in chains.”
Core takeaway:
A vigilant prayer life keeps your spirit awake and your mission open.
--- Slide 25 ---
Walk in Wisdom Toward Outsiders
How you talk matters.
Grace in speech is witness.
Many read you before they read Scripture.
Colossians 4:5–6 (NKJV)
“Walk in wisdom toward those who are outside, redeeming the time. Let your speech always be with grace, seasoned with salt, that you may know how you ought to answer each one.”
Core takeaway:
Your speech is part of your witness—let it be grace-filled.
--- Slide 26 ---
Final Summary and Challenge
Christ is supreme — keep Him central.
Stay rooted; reject anything that replaces Him.
Put sin to death; put on holiness; live prayerfully.
Colossians 3:3 (NKJV)
“For you died, and your life is hidden with Christ in God.”
Core takeaway:
Keep Christ central: stay rooted, reject replacements, put on holiness.
