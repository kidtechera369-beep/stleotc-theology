--- Slide 4 ---
St. Peter: Opening the Door
The Keys:
St. Peter leads the first public preaching at Pentecost and shepherds the early unity of the believers.
Gentile Inclusion:
Through the vision in Acts 10, God reveals that the "medicine & Grace" of salvation is for everyone—no longer restricted by old dietary laws or ethnic heritage.
The Spirit’s Lead:
This was not a human innovation or a political move; it was the Holy Spirit actively guiding the "Hospital" to reach more patient
The Authority of the Keys (Acts 2:14, 38):
"But Peter, standing up with the eleven, raised his voice and said... 'Repent, and let every one of you be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ...'"
How it connects:
As noted, Peter leads the
first preaching
. He uses the "Keys of the Kingdom" not to lock people out, but to open the doors of the Church. He provides the first "Diagnostic and Cure" (Repentance and Baptism) for the thousands gathered at Pentecost.
--- Slide 5 ---
The Spiritual Architect: The Holy Spirit
In the Oriental Orthodox tradition, Acts is often called the "Gospel of the Holy Spirit."
The Descent (Pentecost):
The Holy Spirit descends on the Apostles, transforming them from fearful men into bold witnesses (Acts 2).
Tewahedo (Unity):
The Spirit brings diverse nations (Parthians, Medes, Elamites, etc.) into one body, reversing the confusion of Babel.
Empowerment:
The Spirit provides the "power" for the Apostles to perform miracles and establish the Church.

This fulfills the prophecy that "Ethiopia shall stretch out her hands to God" (Psalm 68:31).

The Eunuch carries the "Medicine" of the Gospel back to Africa before St. Paul even begins his major missions.

--- Slide 10 ---
External Conflict: The Cost of Witness
External conflict served as a "refiner’s fire." Rather than destroying the Church, persecution often clarified its mission and spread the message further.
The Example of Stephen (Acts 6–7):
Stephen represents the ultimate "Bold Witness." His martyrdom proved that the
Grace
given at Pentecost wasn't just for miracles, but for the courage to die for the Truth.
Political Pressure:
The Sanhedrin and Roman authorities tried to contain the "movement." However, as seen in
Acts 8:1
, persecution in Jerusalem actually forced the believers to scatter, inadvertently fulfilling the command to take the Gospel "to the ends of the earth."
The Symbolism of Fire:
In Exodus, God appeared in a burning bush. At Pentecost, that same Divine Fire no longer stays in a bush or a temple; it rests on
individuals
.
The Transformation:
Peter, who denied Christ to a servant girl, stands up and indicts the very leaders who executed Jesus. This isn't just "bravery"; it is a fundamental change in his nature (Ontological Change) caused by the indwelling of the Spirit.
This slide emphasizes that the Church is not a social club, but a "Charismatic" body—meaning it is fueled by the ongoing, active energy of God.
--- Slide 11 ---
External Conflict: The Cost of Witness (Part-2)
The Martyrdom of Stephen: The Blueprint for Courage
Stephen wasn’t one of the original twelve, but he was filled with
Tsega
(Grace).
The Theological Stand:
Stephen’s speech in Acts 7 was a direct challenge to the idea that God only lived in a building (the Temple). He argued that God moves with His people.
The Transformation of Death:
His death proved that the Holy Spirit's empowerment wasn't just for "success" or "healing," but for the
sanctification of suffering
. When he looked up and saw Christ standing (a sign of respect for a martyr), it changed the definition of victory for the Church.
Political Pressure: The "Scattering" (Acts 8:1)
The Sanhedrin thought that by killing Stephen and arresting leaders, they would snuff out the flame.
The Irony of Persecution:
Instead of extinguishing the fire, they stepped on it and spread the sparks. Acts 8:1 notes that everyone except the Apostles fled Jerusalem.
Missionary Expansion:
These "ordinary" believers went to Samaria and Antioch. Political pressure forced the Church to fulfill the Great Commission. Without the "refiner’s fire" of Saul’s early persecution, the Church might have remained a local Jewish sect.
External Witness (Acts 7:59-60):
"And they stoned Stephen as he was calling on God and saying, 'Lord Jesus, receive my spirit.' ... 'Lord, do not charge them with this sin.'"
St. Stephen is the "First Martyr." His death shows that external persecution cannot kill the Church; it only spreads the "fire" of the Spirit. Like Christ, he prays for his enemies, showing the ultimate "health" of a sou
l
--- Slide 12 ---
Integrity vs. Hypocrisy (Ananias & Sapphira)
In
Acts 5
, this couple lied about their donation to the Church.
This story is often misunderstood as being about money. It was actually about
spiritual counterfeit
.
The "Infection":
They wanted the
reputation
of being generous without the
sacrifice
of actually being so. They were performing "holiness" for an audience.
The Consequence:
The immediate judgment served as a "quarantine." It protected the young Church from becoming a place where people could manipulate the Holy Spirit for social status. It established that
Truth is the bedrock of Unity.
Internal Integrity (Acts 5:3-4):
"But Peter said, 'Ananias, why has Satan filled your heart to lie to the Holy Spirit... You have not lied to men but to God.'"
Ananias and Sapphira represent hypocrisy. It shows that the Church’s strength depends on
Truth
.
--- Slide 13 ---
Ethnic Tension & Justice (Hellenists vs. Hebrews)
In
Acts 6
, Greek-speaking Jewish widows were being overlooked in favor of Hebrew-speaking ones.
The Church faced a "systemic" issue: tribalism. Hebrew-speaking Jews (locals) were inadvertently (or intentionally) favoring their own widows over the Greek-speaking (Hellenist) ones.
The Solution of Structure:
The Apostles realized that "prayer only" wouldn't fix a logistics problem. They appointed seven deacons—notably, all seven had
Greek names
.
The Logic of Grace:
They gave the minority group the authority to handle the resources. This showed that
Unity requires Justice.
You cannot have spiritual peace if there is physical inequality.
Structure for Justice (Acts 6:1, 3):
"...there arose a complaint against the Hebrews by the Hellenists... 'Therefore, brethren, seek out from among you seven men of good reputation, full of the Holy Spirit and wisdom...'"
This shows "Ethnic Tension." The Church didn't ignore the problem; they created a structure (the Diaconate) to ensure justice. Unity requires active work and organization.
--- Slide 14 ---
The Doctrine of Unity (The Jerusalem Council)
Acts 15
is the most significant leadership dispute in the New Testament: Do Gentiles have to become Jews (follow the Law of Moses) to be saved?
The Issue:
Legalism vs. Grace.
The Solution:
The Council met, debated, and listened to the Holy Spirit. They landed on a "middle way"—Gentiles were free from the Law, but asked to respect certain traditions to maintain fellowship with Jewish believers.
The Lesson:
Unity is not
Uniformity
. It is "Unity without compromising Truth."
This was the Church's first "Constitutional Crisis." The question: Do you have to
act
like a Jew to
be
a Christian?
The Conflict:
Legalists (The Circumcision Party) argued that tradition and law were the only way to God. Paul and Barnabas argued that the
grace
of the Holy Spirit was sufficient.
The Compromise:
Acts 15:28 contains the famous line:
"It seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us..."
They decided that the core Truth (Salvation by Grace) could not be compromised, but for the sake of
Fellowship
, Gentiles should respect certain Jewish dietary/moral laws.
The Takeaway:
Unity is not
Uniformity
. You don't have to lose your culture, but you must share a common life and common boundaries.
--- Slide 15 ---
The Apostolic Pattern: How We Grow
The Cycle of Healing:
1. Preaching → 2. Repentance → 3. Baptism → 4. Joining the Life → 5. Trial → 6. Multiplication.
Orthodox Synergy:
We do not just "believe" with our heads; we "walk" in the Spirit with our whole bodies through fasting, prayer, and the sacraments.
A Way of Life:
Christianity is "physical therapy" for the soul. It requires our active participation to see results.
The Goal:
We don't just want to be "forgiven" patients; we want to be "transformed" witnesses who help heal others.
The Prescription (Acts 2:38):
"Then Peter said to them, 'Repent, and let every one of you be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins; and you shall receive the gift of the Holy Spirit.'"
How it connects:
This is the start of the
Cycle of Healing
. It begins with a change of mind (Repentance) and a physical washing (Baptism). You don't just "feel" better; you enter the treatment room.
--- Slide 16 ---
St. Paul: The Persecutor Transformed
Paul’s conversion proves that the Holy Spirit can heal even the deepest "infection" of the soul.
The Instrument:
Saul the Persecutor becomes Paul the Apostle—transformed from an enemy of the Church to its greatest "Planter" and "Architect."
The Lesson:
No one is beyond the reach of God’s Grace. If the most "unhealable" patient can become a doctor, there is hope for everyone.
Transformation:
Conversion is not just a change of mind; it is a total "re-wiring" of the person by the Holy Spirit.
The Chosen Instrument (Acts 9:15):
"But the Lord said to him, 'Go, for he is a chosen vessel of Mine to bear My name before Gentiles, kings, and the children of Israel.'"
How it connects:
This is
The Instrument
. God doesn't just forgive Saul; He "weaponizes" his zeal for the Gospel. He becomes the "Architect" who will build the very "Hospitals" (churches) he once tried to tear down.
--- Slide 17 ---
Acts 15: The Jerusalem Council
The Blueprint for Truth:
When conflict or confusion arises, the Church does not rely on "private interpretation" but meets in Council (Synod).
Apostolic Authority:
The Apostles and Elders do not vote based on politics; they discern the will of the Spirit together as one body.
The Canon:
The Council established the "Rule of Life" (Canon). These rules act like a protective fence around the "Heart" (Dogma) of our faith.
The Lesson:
Truth is found within the community of the Church, not in isolation. We stay healthy by staying obedient to the Apostolic decrees.
The Guarding of the Heart (Acts 16:4):
"And as they went through the cities, they delivered to them the decrees to keep, which were determined by the apostles and elders at Jerusalem."
How it connects:
This represents
The Canon
. Once the Council spoke, it became the "Rule of Life" for all churches. It protected the "Heart" (the Dogma of salvation by Grace) from the "infection" of legalism.
--- Slide 18 ---
Oriental Orthodox Perspectives: 3 Anchors
1. The Living Body:
The Church is an organic union, not a social club. Through
Apostolic Succession
, we are physically and spiritually connected back to the Upper Room.
2. Grace as Power (Tsega):
Grace is the "Divine Energy" that allows for
Theosis
. It is the medicine that doesn't just mask the symptoms of sin but actually transforms the patient into the image of God.
3. Spiritual Warfare:
Unity is not a natural human state; it is a gift. We must fight the "sickness" of division (pride, gossip, tribalism) to stay healthy in the Spirit.
The Anchor:
These three truths keep the "Ship of the Church" steady even when the world is in a storm.
The Living Body (1 Corinthians 12:27 / Acts 1:21–22):
"Now you are the body of Christ, and members individually."
How it connects:
In Acts, the Apostles chose a successor for Judas immediately. This shows that
Apostolic Succession
isn't about paperwork; it’s about keeping the "Life" of the Body continuous. The Church is a living organism where the Sacraments act as the "circulatory system," pumping the life of Christ to every member.

The Open Ending:
Acts ends abruptly because the mission is not over. The "Acts of the Spirit" continue today through the Sacraments and service of the Orthodox Church.
The Challenge:
We are the current
branches
grafted into the
Holy Root
. We share the same "Life Force" as Peter, Paul, and the Ethiopian Eunuch.
Our Mission:
We are responsible for writing the "Acts of the 21st Century" through our faith and endurance.
Final Word:
"Accept one another, then, just as Christ accepted you, in order to bring praise to God" (
Romans 15:7
).
The Open Ending (Acts 28:30–31):
"Then Paul dwelt two whole years in his own rented house... preaching the kingdom of God and teaching the things which concern the Lord Jesus Christ with all confidence, no one forbidding him."
